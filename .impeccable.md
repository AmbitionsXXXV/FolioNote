## Design Context

### Users

FolioNote serves independent Japanese learners who discover material through videos, audio, web pages, and short literature. They use the product in short, focused sessions to understand authentic sentences, inspect readings and grammar, shadow audio, save useful language, and return to it through review. Web is the spacious reading and organization surface; Native is the immediate capture, listening, shadowing, and daily-review surface.

### Brand Personality

Immersive, clear, restrained. The interface should feel calm and literary without becoming nostalgic or ornamental, and technically capable without looking like a developer tool. It should give learners confidence that complex language has been made legible without making the lesson feel childish.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use FolioNote's existing obsidian and warm-amber system, Fraunces / DM Sans / Story Script typography, warm shadows, and large but disciplined radii. Favor an editorial reading rhythm, strong Japanese typography, generous focus around the active sentence, and progressive disclosure for linguistic detail. Support both light and dark themes.

Miraa and Oyomi are interaction references for synchronized language layers, token inspection, and semantic grouping—not visual templates to copy. Avoid generic dashboard card grids, decorative gradients, neon-on-black AI styling, color-only POS meaning, and dense permanent controls.

### Design Principles

1. Authentic content before demo decoration: every preview should come from a traceable real source and lead into the learning loop.
2. One sentence in focus: reveal ruby, romaji, POS, dependency, translation, and explanation progressively rather than displaying every layer at equal weight.
3. Meaning is redundant and accessible: combine color with text, shape, or position; preserve keyboard navigation, visible focus, and reduced motion.
4. Share behavior, not components: Web and Native follow the same learning state and commands while using platform-appropriate layouts.
5. Every state has a next action: importing, processing, failure, sparse content, and completed sessions always provide recovery or continuation.
